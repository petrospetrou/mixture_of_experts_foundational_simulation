How to explore Mixture of Experts

No presentation needed. Start with a simple task, see the gate make a guess, learn how an expert gets credit, and then watch the models update.

  1. 1991 · Regression (start here)Use Next → through 8 steps. Move x, compare gate weights with responsibility, then train the model.
  2. 1991 · ClassificationSwitch the tab, click different areas of the chart, and train on an illustrative two-class pattern.
  3. 1994 · Hierarchical MoEOpen the 1994 chapter, follow the two levels of gates, then use E-step → M-step.
  4. Ensemble comparisonCompare a fixed average or majority vote with input-dependent gate weights. Try both left and right inputs.
Keep these three ideas separate
Gate weightHow much influence an expert will have on the output, based on input x before the answer is known.ResponsibilityHow much credit an expert should get after observing the target y or class label and its error.TrainingUse that credit to update experts and gate; not hard top-k selection.

Scientific scope: All datasets here are small, synthetic teaching examples. The 1991 regression shows foundational soft-gating mathematics; classification is a didactic adaptation, and the 1994 hierarchy is a simplified EM implementation. The ensemble comparison isolates the aggregation rule and is not an experiment that trains bagging models. These are not published benchmark results.
